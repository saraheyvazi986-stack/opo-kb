# Complaints Procedure

## Simple explanation

If you have a dispute with OPO — about an executed trade, a withdrawal delay, a customer service interaction, a fee you believe was applied incorrectly, or any other matter — there is a formal complaints procedure you can follow. This procedure is documented in the Client Agreement clause 17 and gives you a structured path to escalate concerns and seek resolution.

A complaint is different from a casual question or a support ticket. It is a formal grievance that triggers specific obligations on OPO to investigate and respond within defined timeframes.

The procedure is governed by Client Agreement clauses 17.1 through 17.21.

## When to use the complaints procedure

The complaints procedure is appropriate when you have a specific grievance about OPO's actions or services, such as:

- A trade was executed at a price you believe was incorrect
- A [[Stop Loss]] or [[Take Profit]] order didn't execute as expected
- A [[Stop Out]] event closed positions at prices you believe were unreasonable
- A withdrawal is delayed beyond what you consider reasonable
- A fee was charged that you believe shouldn't have been
- A bonus credit was removed unexpectedly
- An account was placed in [[Document Expiry / Close-Only Mode]] without proper notice
- A finding under the [[Market Abuse Policy]] resulted in profit confiscation you believe is incorrect
- Customer service treatment you believe was unprofessional or unfair
- KYC documents were rejected without clear reason
- Any other situation where you believe OPO has failed to fulfill its obligations

The complaints procedure is **not** the right channel for:

- General questions about products or features → use regular customer support
- Account setup assistance → use regular customer support
- Tax questions → consult a tax professional
- Trading strategy advice → OPO does not provide trading advice
- Disputes with payment processors (your bank, card issuer, e-wallet provider) → contact them directly
- Issues with third-party services (signals providers, EAs purchased elsewhere) → contact the third party

## How to submit a complaint

The Client Agreement clause 17.4 specifies the submission process:

> The Client shall make any complaint by sending an email to OPO GROUP LLC.'s designated email address for complaints, in writing, with the following information:

Required information for a formal complaint:

1. **Full name and account number** — to identify you and your account uniquely
2. **The transaction or event in question** — including order numbers, dates, times, instruments involved
3. **A clear description of the issue** — what happened, what you believe should have happened, what evidence you have
4. **Your desired outcome** — what specifically you want OPO to do to resolve the complaint
5. **Supporting documentation** — screenshots, transaction confirmations, prior correspondence, anything that supports your version of events

The Client Agreement clause 17.5 indicates the complaint must be submitted within **30 calendar days** of the event giving rise to the complaint:

> The Client should submit a complaint to OPO GROUP LLC. within thirty (30) calendar days of the event giving rise to the complaint. Complaints submitted after this period may be rejected or treated as discretionary.

This 30-day window is important. A trader who notices an issue but takes months to submit a formal complaint may find the complaint procedurally rejected, regardless of merit.

## OPO's response obligations

The Client Agreement clause 17.6 establishes OPO's response timeline:

> OPO GROUP LLC. shall acknowledge receipt of any complaint within five (5) business days from the date of receipt and shall provide a substantive response within thirty (30) business days from the date of receipt.

| Stage                                     | Timeframe                   |
| ----------------------------------------- | --------------------------- |
| Acknowledgment of complaint               | Within 5 business days      |
| Substantive response (decision or update) | Within 30 business days     |
| Extended investigation (if needed)        | OPO must notify and explain |
|                                           |                             |

If OPO needs more than 30 business days to investigate (complex cases, multi-party investigations, need for third-party data), they are obligated to:
- Notify the complainant of the extension
- Explain the reason for the delay
- Provide an expected completion timeframe

## What OPO investigates and how

When a complaint is received, OPO typically:

1. **Acknowledges receipt** within 5 business days
2. **Assigns the case** to an appropriate department (compliance, operations, customer service supervisor)
3. **Reviews relevant evidence:**
   - The trader's account history and transaction logs
   - Market data at the time of the disputed event
   - Internal system logs (quotes, orders, executions)
   - Communications between trader and OPO
   - The specific contractual provisions invoked
4. **Determines a position** — does OPO agree with the trader, partially agree, or disagree
5. **Communicates the decision** in writing within 30 business days
6. **Implements any agreed resolution** (refund, position re-opening, credit, etc.)

**Server Log File priority (clause 17.4):** In any dispute, the Server Log File is the most reliable source of information and has **absolute priority** over other arguments, including the Client Terminal Log File. This means OPO's server records override any screenshots or platform logs the trader may present if there is a conflict between them.

The Client Agreement clause 17.7 provides that OPO is the initial decision-maker and has the right to choose the method of indemnification at its sole discretion:

> OPO GROUP LLC. shall investigate the complaint and provide a written decision based on its investigation. The decision shall be communicated to the Client in writing.

So OPO is judge in its own case at the first level, and determines how any compensation is provided (e.g., account credit, refund, position adjustment). The trader's recourse if they disagree with OPO's decision is described below.

## Escalation paths if you disagree with OPO's decision

If the trader receives OPO's response and disagrees, several escalation paths exist:

### 1. Internal escalation

Within OPO, the trader can:
- Request review by a higher-level compliance officer
- Request review by the OPO regulatory or legal team
- Provide additional evidence that wasn't available initially

This is the path of least friction and may resolve the issue faster than external escalation.

### 2. SVG financial regulator

OPO is licensed under SVG (Saint Vincent and the Grenadines). The trader can:
- File a formal complaint with the SVG Financial Services Authority
- Provide the OPO complaint reference and decision
- Request regulatory review

However, the SVG FSA is generally considered a lighter-touch regulator than ASIC, FCA, or ESMA-member regulators. Outcomes from SVG-level complaints can vary.

### 3. International dispute resolution

For more substantial disputes, traders can pursue:
- **Arbitration** under SVG law (the agreement specifies SVG jurisdiction in clause 28)
- **Litigation** in SVG courts (which is operationally difficult and expensive for most retail traders)
- **Class action** if the dispute involves many similar grievances (also rare for SVG-jurisdiction cases)

The Client Agreement clause 28 specifies SVG law and jurisdiction:

> This Client Agreement is governed by the laws of Saint Vincent and the Grenadines.

This means disputes that escalate to litigation must be pursued under SVG legal system. For traders in jurisdictions with consumer protection laws (UK, EU, etc.), this can be a significant practical limitation.

### 4. Reputation and public channels

Traders sometimes escalate disputes through:
- Public review sites (Trustpilot, Forex Peace Army, broker review sites)
- Social media
- Trader community forums
- Local consumer protection agencies in their country of residence

These channels can pressure brokers to revisit complaints but don't have formal regulatory power. Their effectiveness depends on the broker's reputation sensitivity and the specific facts of the case.

## Disputes that arise during open positions

The Client Agreement clause 17.21 addresses what happens with Stop Loss and Take Profit orders during dispute resolution:

> Once the dispute has been resolved the OPO GROUP LLC. has the right to trigger the Stop Loss or Take Profit in the chronological order in which they would have been triggered if the Stop Out had not been executed.

In plain terms: if a dispute is ongoing about a [[Stop Out]] event, and during the dispute resolution period your [[Stop Loss]] or [[Take Profit]] orders would normally have triggered, OPO can apply them retroactively at the resolved state. This protects both parties — the trader doesn't lose out on legitimate triggers that happened during the dispute, and the broker isn't liable for trades that never happened.

## What complaints generally succeed at

Based on industry patterns, complaints with the best chance of resolution in the trader's favour involve:

- **Clear factual errors** — a wrong fee applied, a withdrawal sent to the wrong account, a duplicate charge
- **Documented system issues** — platform outages, quote feed problems verified by other traders
- **Clear contractual breaches** — OPO not following its own published procedures
- **Strong supporting evidence** — screenshots, third-party data confirming the trader's version

Complaints that historically have lower success rates:

- **Disputes about market execution quality** during volatile periods (slippage during news, Stop Out timing during fast markets)
- **Disputes about the [[Market Abuse Policy]]** application (broker discretion is broad)
- **Disputes about [[Force Majeure]]** declarations (broker has extensive contractual protection)
- **Disputes about bonus mechanics** when the trader didn't carefully read the bonus terms
- **Disputes about withdrawal timing** when the timing falls within the broker's stated processing windows

## Practical advice for filing complaints

1. **File promptly.** Within the 30-day window, and ideally within a week of the event
2. **Be factual, not emotional.** Cite specific transactions, times, and amounts. Avoid characterizations like "OPO is dishonest" or "this is fraud"
3. **Cite the specific Client Agreement clause** you believe OPO has breached. This shows you're approaching the issue as a formal contractual matter
4. **Provide all evidence upfront.** Screenshots of platform displays, transaction confirmations, prior correspondence. Don't make OPO ask for evidence multiple times
5. **State your desired outcome clearly.** Whether you want a refund, a position reopened, a fee waived, or just an apology — say so
6. **Keep copies of everything.** Save your complaint, OPO's responses, and all supporting evidence. If you escalate later, you'll need this
7. **Communicate in writing.** Phone calls with support don't create the paper trail you need. Use email or the formal complaint channel
8. **Be patient but persistent.** The 5-day acknowledgment / 30-day response windows are firm; OPO can sometimes be reminded of obligations they're behind on
9. **Don't accept the first answer if you genuinely believe you're right.** Internal escalation often produces different outcomes than the first review

## Risk warning

The complaints procedure exists and is binding on OPO — but it's not a guarantee of trader-favorable outcomes. OPO is the initial decision-maker on complaints, and the Client Agreement gives the broker broad contractual discretion in many of the situations most likely to trigger complaints (execution quality during volatility, [[Market Abuse Policy]] findings, [[Force Majeure]] declarations). The escalation paths exist but are practically difficult — SVG regulatory complaints have variable effectiveness, and litigation in SVG courts is operationally difficult for retail traders outside the region. Traders should think of the complaints procedure as a real but limited tool. The best protection against needing it is reading the Client Agreement carefully before depositing funds, sizing positions conservatively, and trading patterns that don't push against the discretion-heavy areas of OPO's contractual protections.

## Internal review notes

- Confirm SVG-jurisdiction complaints procedure applies globally, or split when ASIC, FSCA, and Seychelles agreements are obtained. Other jurisdictions may have stricter complaint-handling requirements
- Document the specific email address or web form for complaint submission — currently the Client Agreement refers to "OPO GROUP LLC.'s designated email address" without specifying it. This should be prominently published
- Document historical statistics on complaint resolution — total complaints received, percentage resolved in trader's favor, average resolution time. This kind of transparency builds trust
- Document the specific escalation contact within OPO for traders who disagree with initial decisions
- Document the SVG Financial Services Authority contact information and complaint procedure for traders escalating externally
- Document the specific complaint exclusions per clause 17.17 and 17.18 (currently the Client Agreement excludes complaints about profits from Spike-driven prices, etc.) — traders deserve to know which types of complaints will be procedurally rejected before they invest time in filing them
- Confirm whether the 30-day submission window is strict or has exceptions (illness, account access issues, etc.)
- Consider creating sub-notes for specific complaint types if patterns emerge — "withdrawal complaints", "execution quality complaints", etc., each with the most relevant Client Agreement clauses
- Document the appeals process within OPO specifically — internal escalation paths are described generically here but should be operational specifics
