# Aggressive Behavior Policy

## Simple explanation

The Aggressive Behavior Policy describes how OPO handles clients who behave abusively, threateningly, or unprofessionally toward OPO staff, systems, or other clients. Unlike the [[Market Abuse Policy]] (which concerns trading conduct), this policy concerns interpersonal and communicative conduct.

A client who crosses the line on aggressive behaviour can face restrictions on communication channels, suspension of account services, or full account termination.

The policy is documented in Client Agreement clause 37.

## What the policy covers

The Client Agreement clause 37.1 establishes the policy:

> OPO GROUP LLC. is committed to maintaining a professional, respectful, and safe working environment for its employees, representatives, and other clients. The Company reserves the right to take appropriate action where a Client engages in aggressive, abusive, threatening, or otherwise unacceptable behaviour.

The covered behaviours fall into several categories:

### Verbal abuse and threats

Communications — through any channel — that include:
- Personal insults directed at OPO staff
- Profanity or vulgar language
- Threats of physical violence
- Threats of legal action that are clearly intended to intimidate rather than pursue legitimate grievances
- Threats to harm the company's reputation through targeted defamation
- Discriminatory language (racist, sexist, religious slurs, etc.)
- Repeated hostile or demeaning communications even after warnings

### Harassment patterns

Behaviours that involve persistence beyond normal communication:
- Sending excessive numbers of messages or emails on the same topic after a response has been provided
- Contacting individual staff members directly (via personal email, phone, social media) after being directed to formal channels
- Attempting to contact staff outside business hours through personal means
- Following staff communications across multiple platforms
- Coordinating multiple accounts or other people to contact OPO repeatedly about the same issue

### Disruption of services

Actions that interfere with OPO's operations:
- Deliberate denial-of-service attempts against the trading platform or website
- Attempting to overwhelm support channels through coordinated mass-contact
- Creating multiple accounts to circumvent restrictions on a single account
- Using bots or automated systems to flood communications channels

### Public defamation campaigns

The Client Agreement clause 37.2 specifically addresses:

> Without limiting the foregoing, the Company shall not tolerate the following behaviours: making false or defamatory statements about OPO GROUP LLC., its employees, or affiliates on public forums, social media, or other platforms with the intent to harm the Company's reputation.

The key word here is "false or defamatory." Honest criticism, factual reviews, and good-faith complaints on public forums are not within scope. The policy targets coordinated or factually false reputation attacks intended to cause harm.

## What this policy is NOT meant to suppress

Clause 37 is designed for genuinely abusive conduct, not for legitimate trader expression. The following are explicitly outside the policy's scope:

| Activity                                              | Within policy scope?            |
| ----------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------- |
| Strong, frustrated complaints through proper channels | No — frustration is normal      |
| Negative reviews on Trustpilot or other public sites  | No (if truthful)                |
| Public criticism of OPO's policies                    | No (if not personally targeted) |
| Filing complaints with regulators                     | No                              |
| Disputing trades through the [[Complaints Procedure]] | No                              |
| Using legal channels to pursue grievances             | No                              |
| Sharing your trading experience publicly              | No                              |
| Comparing OPO to competitors                          | No                              |
| Reporting OPO to consumer protection agencies         | No                              |

Traders should not feel intimidated out of expressing legitimate dissatisfaction. The policy targets abusive behaviour patterns, not unhappy customers.

## What OPO can do

The Client Agreement clause 37.3 outlines OPO's possible responses:

> Where the Company determines that a Client has engaged in unacceptable behaviour, the Company may, at its discretion, take any of the following actions:

| Response | Severity |
|---|---|
| Issue a written warning | Mild — first offense for most issues |
| Restrict the Client to specific communication channels (e.g. email only) | Moderate — for repeated harassment |
| Suspend access to certain account features | Moderate — for ongoing issues |
| Place the account in Close-Only Mode | Significant — for persistent abuse |
| Terminate the Client Agreement | Severe — for the worst conduct |
| Report threats or criminal behaviour to law enforcement | Severe — for actual threats |
| Pursue legal action for defamation | Severe — for actual defamation |

The escalation typically follows: warning → channel restriction → service limitation → termination. But for severe conduct (genuine threats of violence, criminal-level harassment), OPO can move directly to termination without prior warnings.

## How OPO distinguishes "aggressive" from "frustrated"

This is a sensitive line. Some practical distinctions from the agreement's framing:

**Frustrated but acceptable:**
- "I'm really upset about this delay and I expect a clear explanation"
- "This is unacceptable and I'm going to file a complaint"
- "I want to escalate this — who is your supervisor?"
- Strong language about the company's policies or decisions
- Persistent follow-ups on legitimate complaints within reasonable cadence

**Crossing into aggressive:**
- Personal attacks on individual staff members
- Threats of violence (even hyperbolic ones — "I'll come down there..." crosses the line)
- Repeated escalation after final responses on the same issue
- Use of multiple accounts or proxies to harass
- Targeting individual staff personally outside official channels
- Coordinated public attacks intended to harm rather than express legitimate criticism

The Client Agreement gives OPO discretion in this determination, but the practical line is well-established in customer service contexts globally.

## What this means for traders communicating with OPO

Practical guidance:

1. **Channel your frustration through proper channels.** A formal complaint through the [[Complaints Procedure]] is much more effective than an angry message to support
2. **Direct anger at the company or policy, not at individuals.** "OPO's policy on X is unreasonable" lands very differently from "[Staff member name] is incompetent"
3. **Use written channels for serious grievances.** Email creates a record. Phone calls can be misinterpreted in either direction
4. **Take breaks if you're escalating quickly.** A 24-hour cooling-off period before sending a hostile email almost always produces a better outcome
5. **Don't make threats you wouldn't follow through on.** Hyperbolic threats ("I'll sue you for everything you have!") often trigger this policy even when no actual intent existed
6. **Avoid contacting staff outside official channels.** Looking up a staff member's personal email or social media to escalate is a clear violation
7. **Express legitimate concerns publicly through honest reviews.** Public criticism is fine; coordinated defamation campaigns are not
8. **If you're going to file a complaint with regulators or law enforcement, do so.** Don't threaten to do it as a pressure tactic — that's the kind of thing that triggers this policy

## How this policy interacts with other policies

The Aggressive Behavior Policy can be invoked alongside other policies:

| Policy combination | Typical scenario |
|---|---|
| Aggressive Behavior + [[Market Abuse Policy]] | A trader engaged in abusive trading patterns who also threatens staff when confronted |
| Aggressive Behavior + [[Complaints Procedure]] | A complaint that includes abusive language toward staff in the complaint itself — the complaint is still investigated, but the conduct is also addressed |
| Aggressive Behavior + Account Termination | When abusive behaviour is severe enough to warrant immediate termination under clause 27 |
| Aggressive Behavior + [[KYC and Account Activation]] | A client whose threatening behaviour also raises questions about identity or fraudulent intent |

## What happens after an account is terminated under this policy

If OPO terminates an account under the Aggressive Behavior Policy:

1. Open positions are closed at then-current market prices
2. The trader is given an opportunity to withdraw remaining funds, subject to standard [[Withdrawal Procedure]]
3. The trader's KYC documentation is retained per Client Agreement clause 12.10 (5+ years)
4. The trader is generally prohibited from re-opening an account at OPO
5. Severe cases (genuine threats, criminal conduct) may include reports to relevant authorities

The trader can pursue a [[Complaints Procedure]] regarding the termination itself, but the practical reversibility of an Aggressive Behavior termination is low.

## Risk warning

This policy is unusual in that it concerns trader behaviour rather than trading behaviour, but the consequences can be just as severe — full account termination. The line between legitimate frustration and aggressive behaviour is determined at OPO's discretion, and traders engaged in disputes can find themselves on the wrong side of that line without intending to. The safest approach is to channel all grievances through formal mechanisms ([[Complaints Procedure]], regulatory channels, legitimate public reviews) rather than direct expressions of anger to staff. Hyperbolic language that would be ignored in casual conversation can trigger this policy when directed at customer service personnel. Traders genuinely victimized by poor service or contractual breaches have legitimate channels for recourse — using those channels protects both the underlying complaint and the trader from secondary consequences under this policy.

## Internal review notes

- Confirm SVG-jurisdiction Aggressive Behavior Policy applies globally, or split when ASIC, FSCA, and Seychelles agreements are obtained. Consumer protection law in some jurisdictions limits how aggressively brokers can terminate accounts for communication-related issues
- Document the specific internal escalation process when staff receive aggressive communications — how is the determination made that conduct rises to the policy's threshold, and who makes that determination
- Document any specific patterns or examples that historically have or have not triggered this policy. Concrete examples would help traders calibrate their communication style without being overly defensive
- Clarify the relationship between this policy and the [[Market Abuse Policy]] when both might apply — which takes precedence operationally?
- Confirm whether the Aggressive Behavior Policy can be invoked retroactively (e.g. for old communications discovered during compliance review) or only prospectively
- Document the warning-before-action expectations more clearly — when is a warning required, when can OPO proceed directly to restriction or termination
- Consider how this policy interacts with negative reviews on public sites — the language in clause 37.2 about "defamatory statements" is broad and could be interpreted in ways that chill legitimate criticism. Clearer guidance on what's covered vs not would reduce trader uncertainty
- Document the appeals process specifically for Aggressive Behavior findings, beyond the general [[Complaints Procedure]]
