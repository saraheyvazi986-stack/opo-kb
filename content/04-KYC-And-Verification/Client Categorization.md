# Client Categorization

## Simple explanation

OPO classifies every client into one of three regulatory categories. The category you're placed in determines what protections you receive, what disclosures OPO must give you, and what kinds of trading you have access to. **Almost all individual traders are — and should remain — classified as Retail Clients.**

The three categories are:

1. **Retail Client** — the default; receives the most protections
2. **Professional Client** — for experienced/sophisticated traders; fewer protections in exchange for fewer restrictions
3. **Eligible Counterparty** — for institutional clients (banks, funds, regulated investment firms)

The categorization is governed by Client Agreement clause 4.

## The categorization is a regulatory framework, not a marketing tier

It's important to understand at the outset what client categorization *is* and *isn't*.

**It is:** a regulatory framework that exists in most major financial jurisdictions (UK FCA, EU MiFID II, ASIC, etc.) to ensure that clients receive protections appropriate to their financial sophistication. The rules are inherited from these frameworks, even though OPO operates under SVG jurisdiction.

**It is not:** a tier system where "Professional" means "better service" or "more features." In fact, Professional categorization usually means **fewer protections** in exchange for fewer restrictions on what you can trade and how.

A common trader mistake is to accept an offer to upgrade to "Professional Client" thinking it's a status upgrade. It is — but it's a status upgrade in the same sense that signing a higher-risk job contract is an upgrade. You take on more responsibility in exchange for fewer guardrails.

## The default is Retail

From the Client Agreement clause 4.5:

> The Client will be categorized and treated by OPO GROUP LLC. as a Retail Client unless otherwise expressly specified by OPO GROUP LLC.

This means: when you open an account at OPO, you are a Retail Client by default. You don't need to do anything special. The classification only changes if:

1. You explicitly request to be re-categorized, and
2. You meet OPO's specific criteria for the new category, and
3. OPO accepts your request

A trader who is never asked to upgrade and never requests an upgrade remains a Retail Client throughout their relationship with OPO.

## The three categories — practical differences

### Retail Client (default — recommended for most traders)

**Who qualifies:** any individual who opens a trading account. The default.

**Key protections retail clients receive:**

| Protection | Source |
|---|---|
| [[Negative Balance Protection]] (cannot owe more than deposit) | CA 15.8 |
| Full risk warnings before placing trades | CA 4 |
| Suitability assessments for certain products | CA 4 |
| Protection from Title Transfer Collateral Arrangement (TTCA) | CA 6.15 (excludes retail) |
| Mandatory dispute resolution under retail-friendly procedures | CA 17 |
| Some limits on cross-product/cross-account fund usage | CA 13 |

**Trade-offs:**
- Standard maximum leverage applies (high-leverage account caps still apply, but no super-high overrides)
- Some products may not be available
- Some account types or features may be reserved for professionals

For most individual traders, Retail is the correct category and should be kept.

### Professional Client

**Who qualifies:** the Client Agreement clause 4.4 sets out a multi-test framework. To qualify as a Professional Client, a trader must satisfy at least **two of three** quantitative criteria:

> (a) The Client has carried out transactions, in significant size, on the relevant market at an average frequency of 10 per quarter over the previous four quarters;
> (b) The size of the Client's financial instrument portfolio, defined as including cash deposits and financial instruments, exceeds EUR 500,000;
> (c) The Client works or has worked in the financial sector for at least one year in a professional position, which requires knowledge of the transactions or services envisaged.

In plain terms, to be considered a Professional Client, you need at least **two** of:
- Active trading history (10+ significant trades per quarter for the past year)
- Significant financial assets (€500,000+ portfolio)
- Financial sector work experience (1+ year in a relevant role)

**Important:** the trader must **request** Professional categorization in writing. OPO does not auto-promote traders based on activity.

**What Professional Clients lose (compared to Retail):**

| Protection lost when becoming Professional                   | Risk to trader                                                          |
| ------------------------------------------------------------ | ----------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Automatic Negative Balance Protection                        | Can owe more than the deposit during catastrophic events                |
| Mandatory pre-trade risk warnings                            | Less hand-holding from the broker                                       |
| Some retail-tier dispute resolution mechanisms               | Disputes treated as commercial rather than consumer                     |
| Default exclusion from Title Transfer Collateral Arrangement | TTCA can apply — client becomes unsecured creditor on broker insolvency |
| Some suitability assessments                                 | OPO assumes you understand all risks                                    |
|                                                              |                                                                         |

**What Professional Clients gain:**
- Access to higher leverage in some jurisdictions (less relevant under SVG, where retail leverage can already be very high)
- Access to more sophisticated products (where offered)
- Streamlined onboarding for new instruments/services
- May qualify for tighter spreads or commission structures

**Why most traders should NOT upgrade to Professional:**

The most consequential single thing Professional Clients lose is **Negative Balance Protection.** During a catastrophic market event (Swiss Franc un-peg 2015, COVID March 2020, etc.), retail clients are protected from owing more than their deposit. Professional clients are not.

Professional Client status is appropriate for:
- Genuinely professional traders with substantial portfolios who understand the risks
- Institutional traders operating individually
- Traders who specifically need leverage or products only available at Professional tier

For most individual retail traders, the marginal benefits of Professional status are vastly outweighed by the loss of Negative Balance Protection.

### Eligible Counterparty

**Who qualifies:** the Client Agreement clause 4.2 limits this category to specific institutional entities:

> credit institutions, investment firms, other authorized or regulated financial institutions, insurance undertakings, collective investment schemes and management companies of such schemes, pension funds and management companies of such funds, commodity and commodity derivatives dealers, locals, other institutional investors

In plain terms:
- Banks and credit institutions
- Regulated investment firms
- Insurance companies
- Pension funds
- Hedge funds and asset managers
- Other large institutional investors

**Key characteristics:**
- Receives the **fewest** protections of any client category
- Subject to TTCA (Title Transfer Collateral Arrangement) — funds may be treated as the broker's assets, with the client as unsecured creditor on insolvency
- Trading occurs on commercial-counterparty terms rather than retail consumer terms
- Disputes handled through commercial arbitration rather than retail consumer protections

**Eligible Counterparty status is essentially never appropriate for an individual trader.** It's for institutions.

## How to check or change your category

To check your current category:
- Log into the OPO dashboard
- Account or Profile section
- Look for "Client Classification" or "Categorization" — most brokers display this prominently

If your category isn't shown, you can:
- Check your account opening documents (which specified your initial categorization)
- Contact OPO support to confirm

To request a category change, the Client Agreement clause 4.3 specifies:

> The Client has the right to request a different categorization, but the Client should be aware that he/she may not enjoy the same protections.

In practice:
1. Submit a written request to OPO support
2. Provide documentation supporting your qualifying criteria (for Professional: trading history, portfolio statements, employment history)
3. Acknowledge the loss of protections in writing
4. OPO reviews and either accepts or denies

OPO is **not obligated to accept** a re-categorization request. The Client Agreement clause 4.3 explicitly:

> The Company is not required to grant a request for re-categorization.

So a trader requesting Professional status to gain features can be denied — even if they meet the formal criteria.

## Why traders are sometimes offered Professional status

Some brokers actively offer Professional Client upgrades to traders who meet (or appear to meet) the criteria. The marketing pitch usually emphasizes:

- "Higher leverage" (often the primary hook in jurisdictions where retail leverage is capped)
- "Lower spreads"
- "Sophisticated tools"
- "Faster execution"

These benefits are real but typically marginal. The cost — loss of Negative Balance Protection and other protections — is large but easy to underestimate until disaster strikes.

In OPO's specific case operating under SVG:
- Retail leverage is already very high (up to 1:2000 on the high-leverage account)
- The leverage-bait for Professional upgrades is reduced
- The case for upgrading is correspondingly weaker for most traders

Be skeptical of upgrade offers. The default Retail status is almost certainly the right one.

## Risk warning

Client categorization is one of the most underappreciated decisions a trader makes — often without realizing they're making one. Retail categorization provides substantial protections (most importantly Negative Balance Protection) that are lost or reduced under Professional or Eligible Counterparty status. The marketing benefits of upgrading (higher leverage, lower spreads, more products) are typically marginal compared to the cost of losing retail-tier protections. Traders should accept Professional categorization only if they genuinely understand the risks, have substantial financial resources to absorb potential catastrophic losses, and have specific reasons to need professional-tier access. For most individual traders, **Retail is the correct status — keep it.**

## Internal review notes

- Confirm SVG-jurisdiction client categorization applies globally, or split when ASIC, FSCA, and Seychelles agreements are obtained. The categorization framework is inherited from EU MiFID II and may need adjustment for other jurisdictions
- The Client Agreement clause 4.4 specifies criteria in EUR (€500,000 portfolio). Confirm whether OPO applies this threshold in EUR specifically, or converts to other currencies — and at what exchange rate
- Document the actual rate of Professional Client upgrades at OPO. If genuinely few traders upgrade, this should be reassuring information traders can verify
- Document the specific products or features available only to Professional or Eligible Counterparty clients (if any). This is currently not publicly clear at OPO
- Consider whether the OPO website should make client categorization more prominent in account opening — many traders don't realize they're being categorized at all
- Clarify the specific dispute resolution differences between Retail and Professional clients beyond general statement that "fewer protections apply"
- Document whether re-categorization is reversible. The Client Agreement is silent on whether a Retail client who upgrades to Professional can later downgrade back to Retail (and whether OPO is obligated to allow this)
