KYC and Account Activation

Simple explanation

KYC stands for “Know Your Customer” — the verification process every regulated broker uses to confirm the identity of new clients before allowing them to deposit funds or trade. At OPO, KYC is a legal requirement, not an optional step. Until your identity and address are verified, your account exists in a limited state with restrictions on what you can do.

The process is governed by Client Agreement clauses 3 (Provision of Services and Initial Onboarding), 20.6–20.9 (Document Status and Account Restrictions), 27.2 (Termination for Misrepresentation), and 24 (Anti-Money Laundering provisions).

Why KYC exists

KYC is required by international anti-money-laundering (AML) and counter-terrorist-financing (CTF) regulations. Every financial institution in the world that accepts client funds is required to:

  1. Verify each client’s identity using government-issued documents
  2. Verify each client’s address using independent documentation
  3. Understand the source of client funds and their intended trading purpose
  4. Monitor ongoing activity for patterns suggesting illicit financial activity

OPO, operating under SVG jurisdiction and license 866LLC2021, is subject to these requirements through both its SVG regulator and the international AML framework that applies to all licensed financial services firms.

Without KYC verification, OPO cannot legally accept your funds or allow you to trade — regardless of how much you’d like to start.

The standard KYC document set

For most individual retail clients, the required documents are:

Proof of identity (POI)

A government-issued photo ID showing your full name, date of birth, and photograph:

  • National ID card (front and back, both sides)
  • Passport (the page with your photo and personal details)
  • Driver’s license (front and back)

The document must be:

  • Currently valid (not expired)
  • Issued by a recognized government authority
  • Clearly readable in your uploaded image (good lighting, no blur)
  • Showing all four corners of the document
  • Original photograph from the document — not a photocopy of a photocopy

Proof of address (POA)

A document showing your name and current residential address, issued within the last 3 months:

  • Utility bill (electricity, water, gas, internet, landline phone)
  • Bank statement (showing the same name as the POI)
  • Tax document (government letter, official correspondence)
  • Rental agreement (signed and dated)
  • Residence certificate (government-issued)

The document must:

  • Be no more than 3 months old at submission
  • Show your full name matching the POI
  • Show your complete residential address
  • Be from a recognized issuer (not a handwritten note)

What does NOT qualify:

  • Mobile phone bills (often excluded because they don’t verify physical address)
  • Credit card statements (sometimes accepted, sometimes not — check with support)
  • P.O. Box addresses
  • Family member’s documents

Additional documents may be requested

OPO reserves the right to request additional documentation based on the trader’s profile, deposit amounts, or compliance review. From Client Agreement clause 3:

The Company shall be entitled at any time to request additional documents or information from the Client for the purpose of verifying their identity, source of funds, or for compliance with any legal or regulatory obligation.

Common additional requests:

  • Bank statements showing the source of deposit funds
  • Employment verification or income documentation
  • Tax residency certificate
  • Source of wealth declaration (especially for larger deposits)
  • Selfie holding your ID (to confirm the person submitting documents matches the ID)

The account states

An OPO account exists in one of three states based on KYC status:

State 1: Account opened but not verified

When you first register and create an account, before submitting any documents:

  • Login works
  • Can see the platform
  • Cannot deposit funds (or, if deposits are allowed, cannot withdraw them later)
  • Cannot place trades
  • Cannot access most account features

State 2: Account verified (active)

After submitting and having documents approved:

  • Full account access
  • Can deposit, trade, withdraw
  • All features available based on account type
  • Subject to ongoing monitoring per Client Agreement clause 24

State 3: Account in Close-Only Mode

If documents expire or new documentation is requested but not provided in time:

  • Can close existing positions
  • Cannot open new positions
  • Cannot deposit funds
  • Cannot withdraw funds
  • Account is functional only for winding down positions

See Close-Only Mode for the full mechanics of this state.

The verification process — typical timeline

The actual KYC review timeline varies by account complexity, document quality, and OPO’s current review backlog. Typical ranges:

StepTypical timeframe
Document submission to acknowledgmentImmediate (automated)
Initial review and decision1–3 business days
Request for additional documents (if needed)Adds 1–5 business days each round
Full verification complete1–7 business days

Faster processing is common for:

  • Clear, high-quality document images
  • Common-jurisdiction clients (countries OPO handles routinely)
  • Smaller initial deposit amounts
  • Straightforward source-of-funds

Slower processing is common for:

  • Higher-risk jurisdictions
  • Larger deposit amounts
  • Discrepancies between documents
  • First-time deposits via unusual payment methods

What can cause KYC rejection

OPO can reject or delay verification for any of the following:

  1. Document quality issues: unclear images, missing corners, poor lighting, partial views
  2. Document validity issues: expired ID, expired POA (older than 3 months), document type not accepted
  3. Name or detail mismatches between POI and POA, or with the registration information
  4. Restricted country residence: see Restricted Countries — citizens or residents of certain countries cannot open accounts at OPO
  5. Negative results in compliance screening: sanctions list matches, PEP (politically exposed person) status without proper documentation, suspected fraudulent identity
  6. Source of funds concerns: inability to verify where deposit funds came from, especially for larger amounts
  7. Multiple accounts: OPO generally allows only one trading account per individual; attempts to open additional accounts may be rejected

When verification is rejected:

  • OPO typically explains the reason
  • The trader can resubmit corrected documents
  • Some rejections are appealable through customer support

What happens if you misrepresent information

The Client Agreement clause 27.2 is unambiguous on this point:

OPO GROUP LLC. shall be entitled to terminate this Agreement with immediate effect by giving Written Notice to the Client where: (a) the Client provides false information or makes a false declaration to OPO GROUP LLC…

Misrepresentation can include:

  • Using someone else’s documents
  • Submitting a forged or altered document
  • Lying about country of residence
  • Concealing politically exposed person status
  • Providing false employment or income information
  • Opening accounts in violation of restricted-country rules

The consequences of detected misrepresentation:

Even minor misrepresentations (e.g. using documents that are technically not the client’s own, even if from a family member) can result in account closure. The protection of the broker’s regulatory standing means OPO applies these rules strictly.

Ongoing KYC obligations

KYC is not a one-time event. The Client Agreement clause 24 establishes ongoing obligations:

The Client undertakes to: (a) provide OPO GROUP LLC. with such information as OPO GROUP LLC. may reasonably require from time to time to verify their identity, address, source of funds, and other personal information relevant to the trading relationship.

Practical ongoing obligations:

  • Document renewal: When ID or POA documents expire, you must submit updated versions
  • Address updates: If you move, you must update your address with supporting POA
  • Identity updates: Name changes (marriage, legal name change) require documentation
  • Source-of-funds updates: If your financial circumstances change materially (large deposit increases), additional documentation may be requested
  • Periodic re-verification: OPO may request fresh KYC documents periodically for active accounts

Failure to maintain current KYC documentation moves the account into Close-Only Mode.

Tax reporting obligations

While not strictly part of KYC, Client Agreement clause 24 also creates tax-related obligations:

The Client acknowledges and accepts that all personal information collected for the purposes of this Section 24 may be disclosed by OPO GROUP LLC. to relevant regulatory and tax authorities as required by law.

OPO may be required to report account activity to:

  • The trader’s country of tax residence
  • The country issuing the trader’s ID
  • Other authorities under international information-sharing agreements (such as CRS — Common Reporting Standard)

This is automatic — the trader does not need to do anything to enable this reporting, and cannot opt out. The reporting is part of the international compliance framework OPO must follow.

Traders are themselves responsible for their own tax compliance on trading profits in their country of residence. OPO does not provide tax advice.

Practical guidance for smooth KYC

  1. Use high-quality document images. A clear photo or scan beats a blurry one every time
  2. Match information exactly across registration, POI, and POA
  3. Submit POA dated within the last 3 months — older documents are routinely rejected
  4. Use original documents, not photocopies of photocopies
  5. Submit promptly after account opening — there’s nothing to gain by delaying
  6. Respond quickly to additional document requests — these don’t go away if ignored
  7. Keep digital copies of all submitted documents for your records
  8. Update documents before they expire, not after — proactive document renewal avoids Close-Only Mode

Risk warning

KYC is not a hurdle to clear and forget — it’s an ongoing relationship requirement. A trader who passes initial verification but later allows documents to expire, fails to update an address, or doesn’t respond to compliance requests can find their account moved to Close-Only Mode without warning. Once in Close-Only Mode, the trader cannot deposit, cannot withdraw, and cannot open new positions — they can only close what’s already open and submit fresh documentation. Recovery typically takes 1–7 days during which trading is impossible. Maintain current KYC documentation as a routine matter to avoid these disruptions. Misrepresentation in KYC documentation, even if seemingly minor, can result in immediate account termination and confiscation of funds — the protection of the broker’s regulatory standing means these rules are applied strictly without discretion for “small” violations.

Internal review notes

  • Confirm SVG-jurisdiction KYC requirements apply globally, or split when ASIC, FSCA, and Seychelles agreements are obtained. KYC requirements vary substantially by jurisdiction, with stricter requirements in many regulated markets
  • Document the specific acceptable document types per country if these vary — currently the agreement uses generic terms (“government-issued ID”) which may have different valid forms across jurisdictions
  • Document typical processing times by deposit amount tier and country — this kind of operational data helps traders set expectations and reduces inbound complaints
  • Document the appeals process specifically for rejected KYC submissions — separate from general Complaints Procedure if it differs
  • Clarify the OPO position on accounts for non-residents who can prove their residency through alternative documentation (digital nomads, retirees living abroad, etc.) — this is a growing trader segment with unique documentation needs
  • Document the specific source-of-funds thresholds that trigger additional documentation requests
  • Document the PEP (politically exposed person) handling process — currently unclear what happens when a PEP applies for an account
  • Confirm the periodic re-verification cadence — currently the agreement says “from time to time” without specifying intervals
  • Document the data retention period for KYC documents specifically (separate from the 5-year general retention in clause 12.10) — KYC documents are particularly sensitive
